CoilTag

Steel coil tag scanning with GPS geotagging: one photo of a coil tag becomes a verified record, pinned on a live map that only authorised staff can see.

1. Abstract

Steel coils are identified by a printed tag carrying the batch ID, size, weight, grade, heat number and specification. In most yards these tags are read by eye and copied by hand into registers or spreadsheets, and a coil’s location is rarely recorded. The result is transcription errors, time lost searching the yard during dispatch, and no evidence when a coil’s identity, condition or location is disputed.

CoilTag is an Android app with a secure cloud backend. A yard worker photographs a coil tag; the app decodes every QR code on it and reads the printed text on the phone itself, so it works without mobile signal. It fills in a structured record, cross-checks the printed batch ID against the QR code, and stamps the record and photo with the phone’s GPS position and time. The worker confirms the fields and saves. Records upload automatically when signal is available and appear on a shared map, where managers can search any batch and see where it is and how it has moved. Access is limited to people the company’s admin adds, with roles enforced by the server.

2. The problem

  • Manual transcription. Batch and heat numbers are copied by hand, and one wrong character breaks traceability.
  • Unknown location. Which yard, bay or stack a coil is in lives in people’s memory, which slows dispatch and stock checks.
  • No proof. There is no timestamped, located photo when a customer or transporter disputes a coil.
  • The QR code alone is not enough. Decoding real AM/NS India tags shows their QR codes carry only the batch ID (plus a QCI “Made in India” verification link on certified coils). Everything else exists only as printed text, so a simple QR scanner captures almost nothing.

3. What the app does

CapabilityDescription
Tag readingDecodes all QR codes on the tag, including small secondary codes, and reads the printed fields: manufacturer, product, batch ID, size, net and gross weight, grade, heat number, specification, manufacture date, pack type and customer.
QR as ground truthThe batch ID is taken from the QR code; the printed batch is cross-checked against it, with look-alike characters (O/0, I/1, S/5) handled so only real mismatches are flagged.
Human confirmationThe worker sees the photo, which can be zoomed, next to the extracted fields and can correct any field before saving.
GPS geotaggingThe position is captured at the moment of the photo, its accuracy is shown, and a weak fix needs confirmation. Locations from fake-GPS apps are flagged on the record.
Photo evidenceGPS, time and “Manufacturer | Batch | Product” are written into the photo. The full-resolution photo is kept in the phone’s gallery and a compressed copy of about 200 KB is stored in the cloud.
Offline-firstReading happens on the phone. Saved scans queue on the device and upload automatically when signal returns, without duplicates.
Live shared mapEvery coil is pinned on a map; scanning the same batch elsewhere draws its movement trail. Search by batch or manufacturer, and tap a pin for details and the photo.
Admin-managed accountsThere is no public sign-up. The admin creates logins, including usernames for staff without email, and issues a temporary password that must be changed at first sign-in. The admin can also reset passwords, remove people and assign roles: viewer, scanner or admin.
Data exportAll scans can be exported to CSV/Excel for reports and existing systems.

4. How it works

#StepWhat happens
1PhotographThe worker photographs the tag with the phone camera, or picks an existing photo.
2ReadOn-device machine learning decodes the QR codes and reads the printed text; no internet is needed.
3CheckThe fields are pre-filled; the worker confirms them or corrects them.
4GeotagGPS position and time are attached to the record and written into the photo.
5Sync and mapThe scan uploads when online and appears on every authorised user’s map.

Technology

  • App: native Android (Kotlin, Jetpack Compose), Android 8.0 and later
  • Tag reading: Google ML Kit, with bundled on-device models
  • Maps: OpenStreetMap
  • Backend: Supabase (PostgreSQL, authentication, private file storage), Mumbai region
  • Security: row-level security on every table; admin actions run in a server function
  • Distribution: private Google Play track (not publicly listed)

5. Security and data handling

  • Only people the admin creates can sign in, and new staff must set their own password at first login.
  • Roles are enforced by the database server, not only by the app, so a modified app cannot read or change data it is not allowed to.
  • Tag photos are stored privately and open only through links that expire after one hour, for signed-in staff.
  • No secret keys are shipped in the app. The master key exists only on the server.
  • Removing a person cuts their access immediately, and their past scans remain on record.
  • Data is hosted in India (Mumbai region) and stays the property of the client.

6. Current status

A working prototype exists today, made up of the Android app and the cloud backend.

  • Tested on two real AM/NS India tag formats (galvanized and HR coil): 10 fields read from a single photo, and all 3 QR codes on the sample tags decoded.
  • GPS accuracy of ±6 to 13 m measured on an Android phone in the field.
  • 29 server-side security and access-control checks, all passing.

Not yet validated: other manufacturers’ tag layouts, heavy daily use, or a full yard’s worth of staff. The pilot in this proposal addresses exactly these.
